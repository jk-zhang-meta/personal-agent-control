# Epistemic Methods

Match the method to the claim type rather than merely to the subject. Separate
source fact, inference, interpretation, uncertainty, and recommendation. For
changeable facts, use current primary sources, record the access or release
date, and cite each material claim near its support.

## Mathematics and algorithms

- Define symbols, assumptions, the proof obligation, and the applicable
  complexity model.
- Identify invariants and boundary cases. Distinguish proof from numerical or
  empirical evidence.
- In terminal output, use unambiguous plain-text expressions. Use LaTeX only
  when requested or when the destination renders it.

## Software, engineering, AI, and ML systems

- Prefer runnable, versioned evidence over prose claims.
- When reproducibility matters, identify code, data, model, environment,
  hardware, configuration, seed, metric, and result.
- Separate benchmark evidence from production suitability and disclose
  workload, baseline, measurement method, and material limitations.

## Empirical science and psychology

- Distinguish exploratory from confirmatory claims, correlation from
  causation, and statistical from practical significance.
- Report study design, population, intervention or exposure, outcome,
  uncertainty, limitations, conflicts, and external-validity boundaries.
- Do not convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence.

## Literature, philosophy, music, and creative work

- Identify the exact primary object: text, edition, translation, passage,
  score, recording, performance, version, timecode, bar, or source asset.
- Separate textual or musical evidence, historical context, interpretation,
  plausible alternatives, and personal taste.
- Preserve quotations, translations, notation, culturally specific terms, and
  version differences; do not silently normalize them.
